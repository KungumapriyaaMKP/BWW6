An Exhibition · மரபின் வேர்கள்

Rooted in Tradition

Exhibit No. 01

Kolam

Drawn at dawn with rice flour, the kolam welcomes prosperity and feeds the ants and birds — a daily act of generosity traced in white geometry.

Exhibit No. 02

Kuthu Vilakku

The five-faced brass lamp is lit to begin every auspicious occasion. Its flames represent love, intelligence, patience, determination and togetherness.

Exhibit No. 03

Nadaswaram

Called the "mangala vadyam", the auspicious instrument. Its soaring voice announces the wedding to the whole street — and to the heavens.

Exhibit No. 04

Thavil

The thunderous barrel drum and the nadaswaram's eternal companion. At the moment of the thali, the "getti melam" crescendo drowns every other sound.

Exhibit No. 05

Kanjeevaram Silk

Woven in Kanchipuram with pure mulberry silk and real zari, a wedding saree is an heirloom — often passed from grandmother to granddaughter.

Exhibit No. 06

Temple Architecture

Towering gopurams and thousand-pillared halls — Dravidian architecture shaped the mandapam itself, where couples still marry beneath carved stone.

Exhibit No. 07

Mallipoo

The fragrance of Madurai jasmine is the fragrance of a Tamil wedding — woven into braids, garlands and the mandapam itself.

Exhibit No. 08

Thamboolam

Betel leaves, areca nut, turmeric, kumkum and fruit — offered to every guest as a blessing and a thank-you for gracing the occasion.

Exhibit No. 09

The Rituals

Kasi Yatra, Oonjal, Kanyadaanam, Mangalya Dharanam, Saptapadi — each rite a chapter of meaning, performed exactly as generations before us.
